1. OVERVIEW

    1.1 Purpose of the BSPD
        The Brake System Plausibility Device (BSPD) is a non programmable, indipendent safety circuit, which monitors the driver's brake pressure and power demand at the same time.
        If occours a situation in which the driver requires high motor power(>5kW) and hard braking(> 30bar) simultaneously for more than 500ms, the device opens the Shutdown Circuit(SDC), removing power from the motor.
        The fault must be latched: if the conditions return to normal, the SDC must remain open, and it can only be reset byshutting down and re-powering the device(No reset buttons according to the task).
        The device also monitors the integrity of both sensors, if one of them gives an out of range reading, the device opens immediately the SDC, without waiting for the timer.
        The design uses only analog and discrete logic components, with no microcontrollers or programmable logic.


    1.2 Design principles
        -Fail-safe output: 
            The relay is powered only while all the components are working correctly. Any loss of power, broken wires or component failure means removing power to the relay, which opens the SDC.

        -Single signal convention:
            From the time stage onward every logic signal is intended "ok" when high and "fault" when low, to guarantee the fail-safe output.

        -Thresholds from the regulated supply:
            All the thresholds are generated by resistor dividers from the regulated 5 V rail, so their accuracy depends on the accuracy of the rail (see the worst-case analysis in section 2.1).

        -Separated Supplies:
            The relay coil are fed through a different 5v branch, in order to keeo the raly and switching noise away from the comparator threshold.

2. DESCRIPTION OF THE BLOCKS
    2.1 Power
        -Requirements: The power block must take an unstable input voltage between 9 V and 17 V and regulate it to
        5 V to supply the whole board.
        
        -Block Overview
        [J1] -> [reverse-polarity diode] -> [TVS] -> [input capacitors] -> [buck converter] -> [5 V rail]
                                                                       |-> [ferrite] -> [+5V_RELAY]

        -Input protection: A Nexperia PMEG6020ER Schottky diode (60V, 2A) is placed in series with the input and blocks the current if the supply wires are swapped(redundand protection). The SMBJ18A TVS diode clamps any possible voltage spikes: its 18V stand-off voltage is right above  the 17V maximum input and its 29.2V clamping voltage is well below the 42V buck converter maximum input voltage. 
        C1 (100 uF, 50 V electrolytic) is placed to damp the ringing between the cable's inductance and c2 when the board is connected, and acts also like a samll energy reservoir, C2 (4.7 uF, 50 V X7R) is placed right next to the converter and supplies its fast switching current pulses.

        - Current budget
        Sensors: 30 mA
        Voltage dividers: 1.5 mA
        Comparators and output pull-ups: 2.3 mA
        Logic: 1 mA
        LEDs (5 * 2 mA): 10 mA
        Relay coil: 28 mA
        Total: = 75 mA

        -Regulator
        A switching buck converter was chosen over an LDO, which would have to dissipate too much power at 17V (see decision.md). The ADI LT8607 was chosen for its large voltage input margin(up to 42V), its  maximum output current(750mA) and its wide frequancy range.
        The board draws about 75mA, which is 10% of the converter rating. With a P_OUT = 0.38W and an efficiency of 85% at 12V, P_IN = 0.44W, the converter dissipates about 0.06W and the input diode about 15mW, which means that no heat sinking is needed.
        R1 sets the frequency to 2 MHz, which is above the AM radio band. At this low load the converter works in Burst Mode, with an output ripple below 10 mV; since this is much smaller than the 50 mV hysteresis of the comparators, no extra filter was added on  the analog supply.
        C4 (10 nF) sets a soft-start of about 3.9 ms, which limits the start-up current. C3 (1 µF) and C5 (0.1 µF) were taken from the datasheet reference design.

        - Output
        The Coilcraft XFL4020-472MEC inductor (4.7 µH) was taken from the reference design. The worst-case peak current, at start-up, is about 0.30 A, well below its 2.0 A saturation current.
        R2 and R3 (1 MΩ and 187 kΩ) set the output to about 4.94 V, 1.2 % below 5 V. C7 (10 pF) across R2 improves the transient response.
        C6 (22 µF X7R) is larger than the 10 µF minimum because ceramic capacitors lose part of their capacitance with the applied voltage; the calculated ripple is about 1.1 mV.
        See the calculation notes on the Power sheet.

        - Relay supply
        A ferrite bead (Murata BLM18KG601SH1D) separates the relay coil supply (+5V_RELAY) from the rest of the 5 V rail, so that the coil current step does not disturb the logic and analog supplies. 
        The coil draws 28 mA, far below the 1.3 A rating, with a drop of only about 4 mV.

        - Status LED
        LED2 (green) shows that the 5 V rail is present (about 2 mA).

        -worst case analysis:
        Tolerances: resistor  ±1%, feedback reference 0.762–0.798 V (datasheet).
        4.94V rail, using voltage partitioner, in the following situation R2_max & R3_min and R2_min & R3_max, the possible outcome is 4,76V to 5,15V.

        -Simulation
        All the pictures of the simulation are in the [sim/power](../sim/power/) folder.


    2.2 Inputs
        -Requirements
        The input block connects the sensors to the analog block. It must remove the noise picked up by the sensor cables without changing the brake and power signals, with a delay much shorter than the 500 ms timer. It must also make a disconnected sensor detectable and protect the comparator inputs if a sensor wire is shorted to a wrong voltage.

        -Block overview
        [sensor] -> [pull-down] -> [RC filter] -> [clamp diodes] -> [analog block]

        -Assumptions
        The sensor outputs are between 0.5 V and 4.5 V.
        The signals change in the tens of milliseconds range, while the noise picked up from the inverter is much faster (kHz to MHz).
        The comparator inputs draw almost no current, so they do not load the filter.

        -RC filter
        The two channels are identical: a 10 kΩ resistor and a 100 nF capacitor form a low-pass filter with a time constant of about 1 ms, so it settles (99.3 % after 5τ) in about 1% of the 500 ms timer.
        Including the 100Ω sensor impedance, the cut-off frequency is about 158 Hz. The fastest pedal movement (about 50 ms) corresponds to about 7 Hz, so the signal passes almost unchanged (99.8 % at 10 Hz), while the inverter noise at 10 kHz is reduced by about 63 times.

        -Pull-down resistors
        A 100 kΩ pull-down resistor is placed on each sensor wire, before the filter, so that if a sensor is disconnected it pulls the input to 0 V, below the 0.25 V window limit, and the BSPD detects the fault.
        Since it is 1000 times larger than the sensor impedance, it changes the signal by only 0.1 %.

        -Input protection
        A BAT54S dual Schottky diode is placed on each filtered signal, with one diode to +5 V and one to GND. If a sensor wire is shorted to a higher voltage, or goes below ground, the diodes keep the comparator input between about −0.3 V and 5.2 V, within the comparator limits.

        -Simulation
        All the simulation pictures are in the [sim/inputs](../sim/inputs/) folder.


    2.3 Analog
        -Requirements
        The analog block must detect when the brake and power signals are above their trigger thresholds (30 bar and 5 kW), and when a sensor gives an out-of-range value, outside the 0.5-4.5 V interval.
        The results are sent to the Logic block as digital signals.
                
        -Block overview
        [Filtered signals] -> [threshold comparators] -> BRAKE_HIGH, PWR_HIGH -> [Logic block]
                           -> [window comparators]    -> SENSOR_OK          -> [Logic block]

        -Trigger thresholds
        Brake threshold:
        The brake pressure sensor is linear: 0.5 V at 0 bar and 4.5 V at 100 bar.
        Its sensitivity is (4.5 - 0.5) V / 100 bar = 0.04 V/bar. To find the voltage at 30 bar, the sensitivity is multiplied by the pressure and added to the 0.5 V offset:
        0.5 V + 0.04 V/bar * 30 bar = 0.5 V + 1.2 V = 1.7 V.
        The comparator must therefore switch when the signal rises above 1.7 V.

        Power threshold:
        The power sensor is linear: 0.5 V at -100 A, 2.5 V at 0 A and 4.5 V at 100 A.
        Only positive currents are considered, since a negative current means that the motor is returning energy to the battery.
        The sensitivity is (4.5 - 2.5) V / 100 A = 0.02 V/A.
        The trigger power is 5 kW, so it must be converted into a current with I = P / V, choosing a battery voltage in the 400-600 V range.
        Choosing 600 V as reference: I = 5 kW / 600 V = 8.33 A, so the trigger voltage is 2.5 V + 0.02 V/A * 8.33 A = 2.667 V. When the battery drops to 400 V, the BSPD trips at 8.33 A * 400 V = 3.33 kW.
        Choosing 400 V as reference: I = 5 kW / 400 V = 12.5 A, so the trigger voltage is 2.5 V + 0.02 V/A * 12.5 A = 2.75 V. At the start of the race, at 600 V, the BSPD would trip only at 12.5 A * 600 V = 7.5 kW.
        Since the BSPD must always trip below 5 kW, 600 V was chosen as reference, and the power threshold is 2.667 V.
     
        -Threshold dividers
        Each divider has a total resistance of about 100 kΩ, so it draws only about 49 µA from the 4.94 V supply.
        The hysteresis (see below) raises the trip point by 1 %, so the divider voltages must be 1 % lower: 1.7 / 1.01 = 1.683 V and 2.667 / 1.01 = 2.640 V.
        The brake divider (66.5 kΩ / 34.0 kΩ) gives 1.671 V, and the power divider (44.2 kΩ / 49.9 kΩ) gives 2.620 V; the power values were rounded so that the voltage stays below 2.640 V.

        -Hysteresis
        The TLV9024 has no internal hysteresis, so it was added to the two threshold comparators with a series resistor R_S (10 kΩ) and a feedback resistor R_H (1 MΩ) from the output to the non-inverting input.
        The hysteresis is about 4.94 V * 10 kΩ / 1 MΩ ≈ 49 mV, about eight times the residual noise after the input filter, so the outputs cannot chatter near the thresholds.
        Brake: trips at 1.688 V (29.7 bar), releases at 1.639 V (28.5 bar).
        Power: trips at 2.646 V (7.3 A, 4.4 kW at 600 V), releases at 2.597 V (4.8 A).

        -Window comparators
        A single three-resistor divider (3.83 kΩ / 90.9 kΩ / 5.11 kΩ) gives the window limits, 4.75 V and 0.253 V, shared by both sensors. Each sensor has two comparators: one goes low above 4.75 V, the other below 0.25 V.
        No hysteresis is needed: in normal operation the signals stay at least 250 mV away from the limits, and the first fault pulse sets the latch.


        -Outputs
        The TLV9024 outputs are open-drain, so each one has a 10 kΩ pull-up to 5 V. The four window outputs are connected together with a single pull-up: SENSOR_OK is high only if both sensors are in range. The unused comparator of each chip has its inputs tied to GND and 5 V. Each chip has a 100 nF decoupling capacitor.
        

    2.4 Logic
        -Requirements
        The logic block must provide a single signal that is high when everything works and goes low when something is wrong (hard braking with high power for more than 500 ms, or a sensor fault). Once low, the signal must stay low until the device is powered off.

        -Block overview
        [BRAKE_HIGH, PWR_HIGH] -> [AND 1] -> [RC timer] -> [Schmitt inverter] -> [AND 2] -> [latch] -> [AND 3] -> [Output block]
                                                                 [SENSOR_OK] -^     [power-on reset] -^

        -Timer
        The timer is an RC network driven by AND 1 and followed by a Schmitt-trigger inverter.
        When hard braking and high power are present at the same time, the AND 1 output goes high and the capacitor starts charging. When its voltage crosses the threshold of the Schmitt trigger, the inverter output goes low (fault).
        The Schmitt trigger is needed because the capacitor charges slowly: thanks to its hysteresis it switches only once, with a clean edge.
        The chosen Schmitt trigger is the Nexperia 74HC14PW-Q100, whose positive-going threshold is 2.38 V at 4.5 V and 3.14 V at 6.0 V. Interpolating at 4.94 V, the threshold is about 2.60 V.
        From the capacitor charging formula, t = 0.75 * τ. The delay was set to 485 ms, just below 500 ms, which gives τ = 0.647 s.
        Since no 1 µF C0G capacitor is available with AEC-Q200 qualification, a 470 nF C0G capacitor was chosen (stable with temperature and voltage), so R = τ / C ≈ 1.376 MΩ → 1.37 MΩ (E96), for a delay of 0.75 * 1.37 MΩ * 470 nF ≈ 483 ms.

        -Sensor fault
        AND 2 combines the timer output with SENSOR_OK, so BSPD_OK is high only if both are high. A sensor fault reaches the latch immediately, without passing through the timer.

        -Latch
        The latch is made of two cross-coupled NAND gates. BSPD_OK acts as the set input: when it goes low, LATCH_FAULT goes high and LATCH_OK goes low. Since each gate feeds the other, the latch keeps this state even if BSPD_OK returns high, until the device is powered off.
        LATCH_OK is used to drive the relay, so that a loss of power or a failure of the logic opens the SDC. AND 3 combines LATCH_OK with READY, so that the relay stays off during the power-on reset.

        -Start up reset
        When turned on the latch could start in a random state, and the sensors filters need a few milliseconds to settle.
        The start up reset is made out of a 1uF capacitor and a 100kΩ resistor, followed by a Schmitt inverter, so that READY is kept low for about 117ms after power up, until the node falls below the negative going threshold of the schmitt trigger.
        The BAS3126 diode is placed to discharge the capacitor quecly after the supply is removed, so that the reset circuit can work again even after a short time.

        -Status LEDS
        The brake and power LEDS (yellow) are driven by two spare gates of the 74HC14, since the open-drain comparator outputs cannot supply the LED current. Each LED is connected between 5 V and the gate output, so it turns on when BRAKE_HIGH or PWR_HIGH is high .
        The fault LED (red) is driven directly by LATCH_FAULT, and stays on after a trip until the device is powered off.

        -Simulation
         All the pictures of the simulation are in the [sim/logic](../sim/logic/) folder.

        
    2.5 Output
        -Requirements
        The output block must open the shutdown circuit(SDC) when the logic block signals a fault, and when everything works fine it needs to stay closed. The relay contactcs switchs only the low-voltage SDC, with a maximum voltage of 24V and 1 A. The whole BSPD is projected with a fail-safe structure, meaning that if something goes wrong the SDC shuts down.

        - Block overview
        [RELAY_EN] -> [gate resistor] -> [MOSFET] -> [relay coil] -> [relay contacts] -> [SDC (J4)]
                                        [+5V_RELAY] -^ 

        -Relay
        The chosen relay is the Panasonic TX2SS-5V-X, which has a 5V coil that draws 28mA.
        Its contacts can switch at 2A 30V DC, twice what required (1A 24V DC), leaving margin for the arc when the contacts open.
        The N.O. contacts are used, so the SDC is closed only while the coil is energized, and any fault that removes the coil current opens it.
        The two contacts are connected in series, so if one of them welds the other still opens the SDC. The N.C. contacts are left unconnected.

        -Coil driver
        The coil is powered by +5V_RELAY, the filtered branch of the 5V rail, with a 10uF and 100nF capacitors next to the realy, in order to supply it when the coil turns on.
        The coil is attached to ground throgh a experia 2N7002BK MOSFET. With a 4,94V on the gate is fully above the 2.1V threshold.
        R28 limits the current when the gate charges, and R29 is a pull-down that keeps the MOSFET off if RELAY_EN is disconnected.
        A BAS316 diode is placed across the coil, to absorb the voltage spike when the coil turns off, keeping the drain voltage below 5.7V.

        -Status LED
        A greeen LED, with its resistor to limit the current, is driven by RELAY_EN to show when the relay is on.
                                    

    2.6 Connectors
        All the external connections use connectors from the same family (Molex Micro-Fit 3.0). Their two main features are the locking latch, which keeps the cable connected even under vehicle vibrations, and the polarized housing, which prevents the connector from being inserted the wrong way round.
        The 2-pin version (43650-0201) is used for the power input (J1) and the shutdown circuit (J4), and the 3-pin version (43650-0301) for the two sensors (J2 and J3). Since some connectors are identical, each cable must be labelled and colour-coded to avoid plugging it into the wrong connector.
